# Theory of Change: Anganwadi Nutrition Counselling Intervention
## Programme: Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS), India

## Problem Statement
Despite decades of ICDS implementation, India carries the world's highest 
burden of child undernutrition. Approximately 35% of children under 5 are 
stunted and 32% are underweight (NFHS-5, 2019-21). Evidence suggests a key 
gap is not food supplementation alone, but caregiver knowledge and feeding 
practices — specifically during the critical 1,000-day window from conception 
to age 2.

## Theory of Change

### Inputs
- Trained Anganwadi Workers (AWWs) with structured nutrition counselling curriculum
- Growth monitoring equipment (weighing scales, height boards)
- Supplementary nutrition (Take Home Ration / Hot Cooked Meals)
- Community mobilisation materials
- Supervisory support and monitoring systems

### Activities
- Monthly growth monitoring and promotion (GMP) sessions
- Individual nutrition counselling for caregivers of underweight children
- Group counselling sessions on infant and young child feeding (IYCF)
- Referral of severely malnourished children to health facilities
- Home visits for high-risk children

### Outputs (measurable, short-term)
- Number of children weighed monthly (coverage)
- Number of counselling sessions conducted
- Number of caregivers attending group sessions
- Number of children referred for treatment

### Outcomes (medium-term, 6-18 months)
- Improved caregiver knowledge of IYCF practices
- Increased exclusive breastfeeding rates (0-6 months)
- Improved dietary diversity among children 6-23 months
- Reduced prevalence of anaemia among children 6-59 months

### Impact (long-term, 2-5 years)
- Reduced stunting prevalence among children under 5
- Reduced underweight prevalence among children under 5
- Improved child cognitive development outcomes

## Key Assumptions
1. AWWs have adequate training and motivation to deliver quality counselling
2. Caregivers attend sessions and act on counselling advice
3. Food supplementation is delivered consistently and consumed at home
4. Counselling is culturally appropriate and in local language
5. Referral pathways to health facilities are functional

## Evaluation Question
Does intensified nutrition counselling by Anganwadi Workers, compared to 
standard ICDS services, reduce the prevalence of underweight among children 
aged 6-23 months over a 12-month period?

## Primary Outcome Indicator
Weight-for-age Z-score (WAZ) — continuous measure of underweight status, 
standardised against WHO child growth standards

## Secondary Outcome Indicators
- Height-for-age Z-score (HAZ) — stunting
- Dietary diversity score (DDS) — minimum acceptable diet
- Exclusive breastfeeding rate (for children 0-6 months)
- Caregiver IYCF knowledge score
